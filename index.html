Paste a text, email, or DM. This tool checks it against a library of manipulation, coercive-control, and reasoning-distortion patterns drawn from clinical and forensic research — then shows you exactly which phrases matched, what each pattern does, and how to respond without getting pulled in.

It describes text, never people. It won't tell you who someone is or what they intended. It gives you vocabulary for something you may already sense but haven't been able to name.

Findings

What this is: a pattern-recognition aid built on published research — Freyd on DARVO, Stark on coercive control, Sweet on gaslighting, Bancroft on entitlement, Gottman on contempt, and standard treatments of informal fallacies. Each finding shows its source.

What this isn't: a diagnosis, a psychological assessment, a lie detector, or evidence about anyone's character or intent. Language is ambiguous and context is everything. A clear result is not proof that a relationship is healthy, and a flagged phrase is not proof that someone is abusive. If something feels wrong, that impression is data even when this tool finds nothing.
